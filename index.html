About the model

trend assumptions and sources by type
  • Silica with silane replacing carbon black lowers tan δ 60 °C by roughly 20–30 % at equal hardness.Supplier technical bulletins (Evonik / Solvay type silica and silane data); Rubber Chemistry and Technology literature on “green tyre” compounds.
  • Silane coupling efficiency rises with dose and saturates near 8–9 % of silica mass. Below about 6 % the filler network stays strong (Payne effect), which raises hysteresis, hardness and wear.Silane supplier dosing guides; published strain-sweep (Payne) studies.
  • Higher vinyl content in S-SBR raises Tg, which lifts tan δ 0 °C strongly and tan δ 60 °C mildly.Polymer supplier S-SBR grade sheets; DMA studies of SBR microstructure.
  • High-cis BR improves abrasion resistance and lowers tan δ 0 °C (wet grip). NR lowers cost and tan δ 60 °C slightly but weakens wet grip.Tyre compounding handbooks; polymer blend studies.
  • Traction resin raises tan δ 0 °C and costs some rolling resistance and wear. Plain process oil softens the compound and lowers Tg.Resin supplier application notes (hydrocarbon / terpene-phenolic type).
  • Sulfur raises crosslink density, which raises hardness and lowers tan δ. Total filler raises hardness and heat build-up.Standard vulcanisation and reinforcement texts.
  • Heat build-up follows tan δ 60 °C and filler loading. Cost uses indicative Indian bulk prices per kg, normalised so the ICE baseline is 100 (about ₹165/kg).Flexometer correlation studies; public raw-material price ranges, rounded.

Every equation is written as the ICE baseline value plus deviations from the ICE recipe. The baseline recipe therefore reproduces the baseline outputs exactly. All coefficients live in one MODEL object in the page source so they can be refitted to lab data.
